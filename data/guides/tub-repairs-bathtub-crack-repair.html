Can You Fix a Crack in a Bathtub?

It is possible to fix a crack in a bathtub. However, the best course of action would depend on the nature and size of the crack.

If it is small and located in an area not affected by water, you may be able to use a two-part epoxy to bond the two ends together and strengthen the area. However, it may be more effective to replace the entire bathtub, depending on how big the crack is, and how much water has been in it for a long time.

How Do You Cover up Cracks in a Bathtub?

Do you have an old bathtub that’s showing signs of age? Cracks in the tub are a common problem, but they don’t have to be a cause for concern. With the right materials and some basic DIY skills, you can easily cover up any cracks and give your old bathtub a fresh new look. Here’s a step-by-step guide on how to do it:

1. Prepare the Crack

Start by cleaning the area around the crack thoroughly with warm soapy water, then allow it to dry completely before continuing. Once it’s dry, lightly sand down the edges of the crack with 200-grit sandpaper to make sure it’s smooth and ready for filler application.

2. Apply Filler

The next step is to apply the bathtub filler. Use a putty knife or caulk gun to put enough filler into the crack to fill it completely and make it level with the rest of the surface. Make sure to wipe away any excess before it dries as this will ensure no unsightly bumps when finished. Allow your filler 24 hours to fully cure before moving on to the next step.

3. Apply Sealant

Once your filler has cured, you can move on to applying sealant. Begin by applying a large bead of sealant along both sides of the cracked surface. Make sure not to leave any gaps in coverage between applications, as this could cause problems down the road. Allow your sealant 48 hours for complete drying prior to using it again normally.

4. Paint over Cracks (Optional)

If you want an extra layer of protection from future moisture infiltration, painting over your sealed cracks is never a bad idea! Use a paintbrush or roller and apply two layers of high-quality bathroom paint or enamel (once each layer has fully dried) over the top of all sealed cracks, paying close attention to the areas where water can easily seep through if you do not put a seal on the cracks.

Most Common Causes of Bathtub Cracks

Bathtub cracks can be caused by a variety of different factors, including age, improper installation, and even damage from dropping heavy objects.  Through our years of bathtub repair service, the most common causes of bathtub cracks include:

  • Improper installation of the bathtub.
  • Old, outdated materials used in construction.
  • Seismic and ground movement.
  • Poor water pressure or overloading the tub with objects.

Types of Bathtub Cracks

Bathtub cracks can be a serious issue, and it’s best to leave the repair work to a professional. At Big Easy Bathtubs, our experienced technicians are trained in all aspects of bathtub repair with any type of bathtub crack such as:

  • Hairline cracks: Characterized by thin, shallow lines running along the surface of the bathtub.
  • Chips and holes: Result when objects are dropped on the bathtub and create both large and small chunks that can break off.
  • Blistering: Occurs when water beneath the enamel coating creates a bubble pushing through to the surface.
  • Diagonal/Stress fractures: These are caused by an uneven weight distribution or pressure on the bathtub resulting in a diagonal crack pattern.
