What Is the Standard Bathtub Size?

A standard alcove bathtub is 60 inches long, 30 to 32 inches wide and roughly 14 to 20 inches tall, depending on the model. An alcove tub is the familiar style that sits between three walls, usually with a shower above it. It is the type most replacement projects involve.

Manufacturer spec sheets show how these numbers play out on real products. Kohler lists its Villager cast iron alcove bath at 60 inches by 30-1/4 inches by 14 inches. For tighter rooms, the same company lists its Seaforth alcove bath at 54 inches by 30-1/4 inches. Kohler also notes on its spec sheets that product dimensions are nominal, which is a polite way of saying the real tub can differ slightly from the name on the box.

Freestanding, drop-in, corner and walk-in tubs do not follow one standard. Their sizes vary widely from model to model, so the spec sheet for the exact tub is the only number worth trusting.

Why Two Inches Can Stop a Bathtub Replacement

A 32-inch tub will not slide into a 30-inch alcove. Tile, backer board and plumbing sit behind the walls, and none of them move easily. That is why the opening in your bathroom matters more than the size of the tub you like.

Older homes add another wrinkle. Bathrooms built decades ago were not always framed to today's 60-inch standard, and New Orleans has plenty of them. A tub that looks standard can sit in an opening that measures 58 or 59 inches from finished wall to finished wall. If you are planning a bathtub replacement, measure the space first and shop second.

How to Measure for a Replacement Bathtub

You need a tape measure, a notepad and about ten minutes. Take every measurement in inches and write down fractions instead of rounding.

1. Length of the opening

Measure wall to wall along the back of the tub, at the height of the tub rim. Do this at the left end, the middle and the right end, then keep the smallest number. Walls are rarely perfectly straight, and the tub has to clear the tightest point.

What you are measuring here is the finished opening, tile to tile. The framing behind the walls is wider, often by an inch or more. A new tub usually fastens to that framing, which is one reason a full replacement involves opening up some of the wall.

2. Width, from the back wall to the front edge

Measure from the finished back wall to the outside face of the tub's front panel, called the apron. This tells you whether your alcove was built for a 30-inch or a 32-inch tub.

3. Height, from the floor to the rim

Measure from the bathroom floor to the top of the tub rim. Height affects how easy the tub is to step into and where the tile or wall surround will meet the new rim. A taller tub can leave a gap in old tile, and a shorter one can expose unfinished wall.

4. Drain side and drain position

Stand facing the tub as if you were about to step in. If the drain and faucet are on your left, you have a left-hand tub. If they are on your right, it is a right-hand tub. Alcove tubs are sold in both versions, and the two cannot be swapped.

Then measure from the back wall to the center of the drain, and from the end wall to the center of the drain. The new tub's drain should land close to the same spot. If it does not, the drain line under the floor has to be moved, which adds plumbing work to the job.

5. The path into the bathroom

Measure the bathroom doorway, the hallway and any tight corner on the way in. A one-piece tub has to travel through all of them, and it is an easy step to forget.

Measurements People Forget

  • Weight. Kohler's 60-inch Villager weighs 316 pounds empty and holds 33 gallons, which adds about 275 pounds of water. Upper floors and older framing deserve a look before a cast iron tub goes in.
  • Ceiling and shower height. If a shower rod or glass door goes above the tub, check the height you have to work with.
  • Window and niche positions. A taller rim can run into a low window sill or a soap niche.
  • The spec sheet. Before you order, compare your numbers with the manufacturer's dimension drawing for that exact model.

What If Your Space Is Not a Standard Size?

A non-standard opening still leaves you with choices. Which one makes sense depends on how far off the measurements are.

  • Choose a compact tub. A 54-inch alcove model suits openings that are too short for a 60-inch tub.
  • Adjust the alcove. Framing can sometimes be changed to accept a standard tub. This is a bigger job that involves the walls and often the plumbing.
  • Keep the tub you have. If the existing tub is sound and only looks worn, refinishing restores the surface without changing the size at all.

The Bottom Line on Standard Bathtub Size

The standard bathtub size is a starting point. Most alcove tubs are 60 inches long and 30 to 32 inches wide, but your bathroom has the final say. Measure the length in three places, check the width and the rim height, note which side the drain is on, and confirm the tub can get through the door.
