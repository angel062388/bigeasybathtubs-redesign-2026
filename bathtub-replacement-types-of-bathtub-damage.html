Types of Bathtub Damage – How to Identify and Repair Them

Big Easy Bathtubs is your go-to source for all bathtub repair needs. We specialize in identifying and repairing all types of bathtub damage. From cracks and chips to discoloration and rust, our team of experienced professionals is here to help you get your tub back into its original condition.

Let’s take a look at some of the common types of bathtub damage and how to repair them.

Scratches and Chips

Scratches and chips are common types of bathtub damage that can be caused by abrasive cleaning products or by dropping heavy objects on the surface. While these types of damage may seem minor, they can eventually lead to more significant issues if left untreated. Scratches and chips can make it difficult to clean your bathtub, as they can trap dirt and grime, leading to unsightly stains and discoloration.

Additionally, these types of damage can weaken the structure of your bathtub and create a breeding ground for bacteria and other harmful microorganisms.

Fortunately, there are several ways to fix scratches and chips on your bathtub surface. Small chips can be filled in with a special bathtub repair kit that can be found at most hardware stores. For larger chips or scratches, you may need to hire a professional to sand down and refinish the damaged area.

Stains and Discoloration

Stains and discoloration are another common type of bathtub damage that can be caused by hard water, soap scum buildup, or mold and mildew growth. These stains can be unsightly and difficult to remove, making your bathtub look dirty and uninviting.

Hard water stains are caused by minerals that are present in the water, which can leave behind a white, chalky residue on the surface of your bathtub. Soap scum buildup can create a dull, hazy film on your bathtub surface, while mold and mildew growth can result in black or green stains that are both unsightly and potentially harmful to your health.

To remove stains and discoloration from your bathtub, there are several cleaning methods you can try. For hard water stains, you can use a mixture of white vinegar and baking soda to dissolve the minerals and restore shine to your bathtub surface. For soap scum buildup, a solution of dish soap and warm water can help to break down the buildup and restore shine to your bathtub. To remove mold and mildew, a mixture of bleach and water can be used to kill the spores and prevent further growth.

Cracks and Holes

Cracks and holes are a serious type of bathtub damage that can compromise the structural integrity of your bathtub and lead to water leaks, which can cause further damage to your home. Cracks and holes can be caused by impact damage, improper installation, or shifting of the foundation beneath your bathtub. Small cracks and holes may be difficult to spot, but can quickly worsen over time and cause major damage to your bathtub.

If you notice cracks or holes in your bathtub, it’s important to address them promptly to prevent further damage. Small cracks or holes can be repaired using waterproof epoxy or putty, which can be found at most hardware stores. For larger cracks or holes, it may be necessary to replace the affected area of your bathtub or to replace the entire bathtub altogether. If you are unsure how to repair cracks or holes in your bathtub, it’s best to hire a professional to ensure that the repair is done correctly and safely.

Rust and Corrosion

Rust and corrosion are other common type of bathtub damage that can be caused by exposure to moisture and oxygen. Rust is a reddish-brown coating that forms on the surface of the metal, while corrosion refers to the gradual deterioration of materials due to chemical reactions. Rust and corrosion can be unsightly and can weaken the structure of your bathtub, making it more prone to damage and leaks.

To prevent rust and corrosion from occurring in your bathtub, it’s important to keep the surface clean and dry. Avoid leaving damp towels or bathmats on the surface of your bathtub, as these can trap moisture and promote rust and corrosion. If you notice any signs of rust or corrosion on your bathtub, it’s important to address them promptly to prevent further damage. Small areas of rust or corrosion can be sanded down and repainted using waterproof paint or coating. For larger areas of rust or corrosion, it may be necessary to replace the affected area of your bathtub or to replace the entire bathtub altogether.

Leaks and Water Damage

Leaks and water damage are another serious type of bathtub damage that can cause significant damage to your home. Leaks are caused by worn-out caulking, damaged pipes, or cracks in your bathtub surface. Water damage can be caused by leaks that go unnoticed or untreated, which can lead to mold and mildew growth, rotting wood, and damage to your walls and flooring.

Should you spot any signs of leakage in your bathtub, like water spots or dampness near the bottom, it is essential to address them immediately before further destruction occurs. Small leaks can often be repaired by reapplying caulk or replacing damaged pipes. Larger leaks or damage to your bathtub surface may require more extensive repairs or even bathtub replacement. If you are unsure of how to fix leaks or water damage in your bathtub, it is advisable to enlist the services of an expert. This ensures that the repair is properly executed and performed with utmost safety.

Mold and Mildew

Mold and mildew are common types of bathtub damage that can cause health problems and be unsightly. Mold and mildew thrive in warm, damp environments, making bathtubs a prime location for their growth. These fungi can cause respiratory problems and aggravate allergies, making it important to address them promptly.

To prevent mold and mildew from growing in your bathtub, it’s important to keep the surface clean and dry. After taking a bath or shower, wipe down the surface of your bathtub with a towel to remove any excess moisture. If you recognize any signs of mold or mildew – black, green spots, etc. – it is essential to handle them immediately in order to stop the spread of further growth. Small areas of mold or mildew can often be removed using a solution of bleach and water, while larger areas may require professional cleaning or even bathtub replacement.
