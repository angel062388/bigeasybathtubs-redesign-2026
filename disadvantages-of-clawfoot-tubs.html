Unveiling the Drawbacks: Disadvantages of Clawfoot Tubs

Clawfoot tubs have been around for centuries and are still popular today as a statement piece in many bathrooms.

These free-standing tubs add a touch of elegance and luxury to any space, but they are not without their drawbacks. In this article, we will discuss the disadvantages of clawfoot tubs, specifically those offered by Big Easy Bathtubs.

From their size and weight to their maintenance and practicality, there are several reasons why a clawfoot tub may not be the best choice for your bathroom. So, if you’re considering a clawfoot tub for your home, read on to discover what you need to know before making your purchase.

Big Easy Bathtubs is known for its quality and craftsmanship, but even the best clawfoot tubs have their drawbacks.

Here are some of the most common disadvantages you should consider:

Price and Cost of Installation

One of the most obvious disadvantages of clawfoot tubs is the price. These fixtures are typically more expensive than other types of tubs due to their size and materials used in their construction.

The cost for installation is also higher because a plumber must take extra time and care when installing a free-standing tub, as opposed to simply setting a drop-in tub.

Weight and Size Considerations

Another disadvantage to consider is the size and weight of clawfoot tubs. These fixtures are quite large and usually require special installation techniques, such as bolting them directly to the floor.

This can be difficult in smaller bathrooms, especially those with limited space. Also, these tubs are quite heavy, making installation even more difficult.

Maintenance and Cleaning Challenges

Clawfoot tubs can also be harder to clean than other types of tubs. The design of the fixture makes it difficult to access all areas when cleaning, which means dirt and grime can accumulate over time. Also, these fixtures require a bit more maintenance than other types of tubs due to their porcelain material, which can chip and crack over time.

Limited Showering Options

One of the biggest disadvantages of clawfoot tubs is the lack of showering options. Many people prefer to install a combination tub and shower, but this isn’t possible with these fixtures since they must remain free-standing. This means you will need to purchase a separate shower fixture for your bathroom if you want to enjoy both soaking and showering.

Higher Water Usage and Longer Fill Time

Clawfoot tubs are also not the most efficient option when it comes to water usage and fill time. These fixtures typically have a larger capacity than other types of tubs, which means they require more water to fill. Also, due to their size and shape, they take longer to fill than other types of tubs. So if you’re looking for a quick, easy way to bathe, this may not be the best option.

Slippery Surface and Safety Concerns

Another disadvantage to consider is the slippery surface of these tubs. The glossy finish and rounded edges can make it difficult to get in and out of the tub safely, especially for those with mobility issues. This means you may need to invest in slip-resistant mats or other accessories to ensure your safety when bathing.

Lack of Versatility

Clawfoot tubs are often not as versatile as other types of fixtures, such as drop-in or corner tubs. They cannot be easily moved around the bathroom, and they cannot be converted into showers. This means that if you want to switch to a shower later on, you’ll have to replace the tub with something else.

Limited Accessibility for People With Mobility Issues

Clawfoot tubs may not be the best choice for those with mobility issues due to their size and weight. For instance, it can be difficult for someone with a disability or limited mobility to climb in and out of the large tub. This means you may need to invest in additional accessories that make bathing easier, such as grab bars and handheld shower heads.

Is a Clawfoot Tub Right for You?

Now that you know the disadvantages of clawfoot tubs, it’s time to decide whether they are right for your bathroom. If these drawbacks don’t weigh too heavily on your decision, then a clawfoot tub from Big Easy Bathtubs might be perfect for you.
